# Indian bank peer benchmark, FY2025-26

A like-for-like benchmark of nine listed Indian banks for the year to 31 March 2026, built in Excel from public results documents.

Private: HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, IndusInd Bank.
Public: State Bank of India, Bank of Baroda, Punjab National Bank, Indian Bank.

The point of the exercise was less the league table than the work behind it. The banks publish similar-sounding ratios on different bases, so each metric is rebuilt here on one definition, and every place where a bank's own number could not be used as published is written down in an inconsistency log.

## What is in the workbook

`India_Bank_Benchmark_FY26.xlsx`

- **Inputs**: figures as each bank reports them, in Rs crore, with a cell comment on anything derived or taken from a secondary source.
- **Definitions**: the formula used for each metric, why that basis, and how the nine banks report it themselves.
- **Calculations**: seven metrics on one basis: cost-to-income (with disclosed one-offs removed), cost-to-assets, CASA ratio on total deposits, NII / average assets, ROA, gross NPA ratio and business per branch.
- **Rankings**: quartile position and rank on each metric (Q1 is always the best quartile).
- **Gap to top quartile**: what each bank would gain in Rs crore by moving to the top-quartile threshold on one metric.
- **Inconsistency log**: 29 entries, with the size of each difference where it can be measured.
- **Digital disclosure**: why digital share of transactions could not be benchmarked.
- **Sources**: 42 documents, marked primary or secondary.
- **Checks**: 16 tie-outs and reconciliations. All pass.

Everything outside Inputs is a formula, so changing an input flows through the rankings and gaps.

## Findings

1. **The basis changes the ranking.** SBI reports a CASA ratio of 39.5%, on domestic deposits. On total deposits it is 37.9%. Bank of Baroda falls from 38.9% to 33.1% because 15% of its deposits are overseas. ICICI headlines a quarterly average CASA ratio of 38.6%; its period-end ratio, the basis most peers use, is 41.4%.
2. **One-offs matter.** HDFC Bank's full-year cost-to-income is 38.0% as published and 39.5% after removing a Rs 9,179 crore gain from selling HDB Financial shares and a Rs 800 crore New Labour Code charge. The 39.5% matches the "core" figure in broker coverage.
3. **Cost or margin?** PNB is in the top quartile on cost-to-assets (1.62%) and the bottom quartile on cost-to-income (51.8%). It spends little per rupee of assets. Its problem is a thin margin: NII is 2.16% of average assets, the lowest of the nine.
4. **Size of the gap.** At the top-quartile cost-to-income of 46.0%, SBI's annual costs would be about Rs 10,000 crore lower. Across the peer group the gap is about Rs 21,900 crore. Part of the public sector gap reflects pension costs and loan mix, so this shows scale, not a target.
5. **Averaging method moves ROA.** HDFC reports ROA of 1.94% on average balances. On the average of the two year-end balances it is 1.80%, because the balance sheet grew sharply in the final quarter.
6. **Digital adoption cannot be compared from public documents.** None of the results releases reviewed gives a share of transactions by digital channel on a stated definition. HDFC's audited "Digital Banking" segment covers its RBI Digital Banking Units (Rs 10 crore of revenue) and says nothing about channel migration.

## Limits

Public disclosures are no substitute for data collected from each bank on common definitions. Some inputs come from news feeds, broker notes or data aggregators where a primary document could not be read; each is flagged in the workbook. Business per branch is missing for Bank of Baroda and Indian Bank, whose FY26 documents reviewed here give no branch count. New Labour Code charges are removed for HDFC only.
